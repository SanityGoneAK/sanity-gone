## Introduction

Heya all, Mal here with another Modules writeup! This time around is the “Thunder in the Azure Dream” event patch. This is the second Monster Hunter collab event, and as someone who loved using the bow in every MonHun game I’ve played, I’m thrilled we got a bow character. 

This patch adds Modules for the new MonHun characters, as well as several second Modules for existing Operators (including Kirin S Yato, the previous MonHun Operator).

The new units are Violet Mitzutsune Orchid and Zinogre S Catapult. Orchid2 is a pretty cool unit. I’ve long loved the “Medium Redeploy” (MRD) nature of the Heavyshooter X Module. Many people thought Pozy made her irrelevant when she released, but I thought Schwarz remained competitive for a long time after that because of the versatility of her MRD module. However, Orchid2 blows the old competition out the water. Not only does she boast faster redeployment timing, she has some neat positioning flexibility, great damage, and, as per usual for a 2026 Arknights unit, Take Off. Anyway, her Module is a crucial component of this entire dynamic. It provides a huge chunk of her Redeployment Time reduction, as well as a great amount of deployment positioning flexibility. Her Module level 1 especially is basically mandatory, though her upgrades are good too.

Catapult’s Alter on the other hand is fairly underwhelming. It feels like HG hasn’t hit the balance with the Mercenary Branch. If you want to tank your DP economy Catapult2 can do serviceably decent damage in a quick burst… But the damage really isn’t *that* impressive, especially not considering the cost. It’s a pity, I know he wasn’t “meta” but personally I thought Rathalos S Noir Corne was at least a fun unit who had some interesting gimmicks. Instead, Catapult2 just gets “some damage” with no notable niche to speak of. All that said, if you do insist on using her, her Module is fairly helpful for her damage. It helps her damage go from “mid” to “decent” by 5★ standards.

As for existing Operators, Kirin R Yato, Narantuya, and Vulpisfoglia all got second Modules. Yato’s second Module is pretty great overall. It’s better in almost every way than her first, but that one factor where it isn’t is the DP economy her first offers. I really like this dynamic because it’s an actually interesting tradeoff where people who aren’t the most min-maxing Yato fans might still actually consider having both and swapping between the two. But in general, this one is definitely better, providing substantial damage improvements, and even allows her to be deployed again instantly with no Redeployment timer. Yato definitely doesn’t feel as “broken” as she did when she first released, given the evolution of the game, but I think this new Module helps her keep up.

Narantuya’s Module has even more nuance. For me, this is absolutely the one I was waiting for, however for many others this one will be an easy skip. It all really depends on how you use her. Narantuya’s first Module very much leaned into the Loopshooter “hit close enemies faster” gimmick, and if you’re in those conditions, it will always be better. However, this one goes in the opposite direction. This Module rewards her with extra ATK for not getting hit, and doesn’t add an extra “hit nearby enemies” condition. The result is that if you’re using S1 or S2 at range, this Module is better. Meanwhile, if you’re using S3 at close range, her first one is better. S2 has always been my favorite Narantuya skill, so the addition of this Module is great for me. All that said, Narantuya definitely feels like a “balanced” unit these days in a room full of “broken” units (not the least of whom is the one releasing this patch), and this Module is really more of a situational sidegrade to her first. As a result, this Module doesn’t really do much to help Narantuya keep abreast of the powercreep we’ve been seeing in recent years.

Lastly, we have Vulpisfoglia. When she released, I warned everyone about her Module. This is one of those cases where it honestly didn’t even matter what the Talent upgrades did, the Trait upgrade alone was enough to know the second one would be the better Module. And that is absolutely the case. Minus four DP cost for the first deployment makes this Module a straightforward upgrade over her first Module. While the Talent upgrades are also nice, they’re not nearly as urgent as the Trait upgrade. Her global DP generation Talent is improved, but not by that much. She gets some extra Camouflage, but it’s typically rendered less important by the fact that her S3 gives her Camouflage already. For those still using Vulpisfoglia, this Module makes her a much better Vanguard than before. The only reason to stick with the other Module is if you were particularly invested in Vulpisfoglia’s performance as a pseudo-laneholder. All that said, not to sound like a broken record, but Vanguards have not been immune to powercreep. The “Big Four” of Vanguards: Ines, Silverash2, Closure, and Muelsyse¹ all have an immense amount of wide ranging utility that Vulpisfoglia struggles to compete with. For those of you still using her, you will absolutely still want to get this Module, but I completely understand if many of you have moved on.

¹: they let Andy Murray into the tennis big four! Let me have this!


## Ratings System

This rating system aims to capture both the relative “meta value” of a unit as well as the strength of a Module. My aim is to have the ratings give an at a glance understanding for those just trying to figure out what is “good”, while those already interested in a unit regardless of the meta value of said unit, can read the detailed write-up of each Module to find out if it's right for them, even if it's a Module on a relatively low value Operator.

Each Module will be given a rating of [YES], [MAYBE], or [NOT RECOMMENDED] for whether it is worth building, as well as a recommended level to upgrade it to.

For Specialized Modules, ratings are given with the assumption that players are interested in the applicable game mode (for example Integrated Strategies).

## Ratings

### New Operators

#### Violet Mizutsune Orchid

<ModuleRecommendation moduleType="X" charId="char_1048_orchd2" recommended={true} recommendedLevel={3} client:load/>

Violet Mizutsune Orchid (Orchid2) is the latest in the “Medium Redeploy” (MRD) Sniper niche while also having substantial survivability built into her kit via the Take Off characteristic. Nearly every element of her kit encourages players to frequently redeploy her in strategic positions. Her Module is no different, further improving that niche, giving players even more flexibility and rewards for leaning into this playstyle.

The Trait upgrade of this Module reduces Orchid2’s Redeployment Time by 25s. Before any Modifiers are applied, Orchid2 has a base redeployment time of 70s. However, her Talent 2 (with no Module) reduces that by 15s down to 55s. This Trait upgrade further reduces that time down to 30s. As a MRD unit, these redeploy times functionally serve as Orchid2’s skill cycle times. As a result, this can result in substantially faster cycles (the exact timing depends on skill use). This upgrade should be the absolute first priority for players to build on Orchid2, perhaps even more important than mastery. In fact, while this guide calls her an MRD unit, this upgrade arguably turns her into a full fledged Fast Redeploy (FRD) unit.

The Talent upgrades of this Module improve Orchid2’s Redeploy Talent. First of all, the Redeployment Time reduction is increased to 18s (up from 15s) resulting in a total of 27s Redeployment Time. Secondly, Orchid2’s ATK buff for redeploying nearby her original position is increased to +20% ATK (up from 15%) for 30s. Last but not least, the definition of what constitutes “nearby” is expanded. Originally, Orchid2 needed to be redeployed within a Texas S2 shaped range, however at Module level 3 she gets this buff whenever redeployed within a Saria S2 shaped range. The redeploy and damage upgrade portions of this Talent improvement are nice but the main attraction is the increased range for the Talent condition. This allows much more flexible positioning of Orchid2 to maintain her buffs (as well as expanding the number of Melee tiles on which she can be deployed).

Orchid2 is a unit who rewards players for actively engaging with her positioning and redeployment. This Module leans into that playstyle in multiple ways. Orchid2 is essentially half a unit without the upgrades of this Module, and it should be considered high priority for any player planning to build her. 


#### Zinogre S Catapult

<ModuleRecommendation moduleType="X" charId="char_1049_catap2" recommended={false} client:load/>

Zinogre S Catapult (Catapult2) is an iteration on the Mercenary Guard Branch. The theory is that players can spend large amounts of DP to enhance her damage. However, the actual DPS output of this unit is simply underwhelming, even by 5★ standards. As a result, the “tradeoff” ends up simply being the requirement to do even serviceable damage. This Module provides damage improvements to Catapult’s kit, but those improvements are simply not enough to make her stand out compared to her similar rarity competitors, much less when compared to 6★ units.

The Trait upgrade of this Module grants Catapult2 a stacking ATK mechanic. Whenever she consumes DP, each point increases her ATK by +2% for the skill duration, up to 10 stacks. Her S1 can max out these stacks with one normal activation, consuming 20 DP to hit the maximum +20% ATK from this Trait. Her S2 can use “Equipment Activation” to consume multiple charges of the skill, do more damage, and consume more DP, maxing out the stacks of this buff at +20% ATK.

The Talent upgrades of this Module improves her charge consumption Talent. In addition to increasing the duration (this buff is unchanged from Module level 0), each charge consumed will also increase Catapult2’s ATK by +3% per charge (for a maximum of +15% ATK). It should be noted that her more commonly used skill, S2, already has a large +ATK% buff contained within it, and as result these buffs aren’t that impactful.

This Module is peculiar, on the one hand it provides a decent damage buff to Catapult2, on the other hand, even with that buff she simply doesn’t compete very well with her competitors. Her ability to spam these DPS skills is distinct in her rarity, but the DP costs of doing so are lavish and there are many 5★ units who can generally do more without the DP consumption downsides. Those who insist on using Catapult2 will probably notice the benefits, but most players probably shouldn’t build her.


### Second Modules


#### Kirin R Yato

<ModuleRecommendation moduleType="Y" charId="char_1029_yato2" recommended={true} recommendedLevel={3} client:load/>

Kirin R Yato (Yato2) combines a variety of factors that make her an often indispensable unit. She can hit in unique range, perform “true” AoE damage, perform many hits, and be intangible while outputting this damage. Furthermore, she can do all this on an incredibly quick cycle, be retreated, and then a very fast cooldown later, do it again. While Yato2 already had a good Module, this second Module improves her in *almost* every way compared to the first.

The Trait upgrade of this Module grants Yato2 +10% ATK when there are no allied units in the four adjacent tiles. While there is a condition, when it is met, this Trait upgrade by itself almost entirely matches all the damage buffs present in Yato2’s first Module. The condition is fairly easy to accomplish for FRD units, as they are often deployed in dangerous positions where normal units wouldn’t be safe. A simple but substantial increase. Giving up the Trait upgrade of her 1st Module (Module X), the DP refund upon retreat, is probably the only downside of this Module. 

The Talent upgrade of this Module improves Yato2’s Talent 1. First, the extra component of Arts damage Yato2 gets on each hit is increased to 25% ATK (up from 20%). Second, a new feature is added: when Yato2 deals Arts damage (in other words with every hit she performs), she gains a personal Arts Damage Amplification of 5%, stacking up to 10 times for 50%. Third, the first time Yato2 is retreated, her Redeployment Time is reduced to 0, allowing her to be instantly redeployed. The stacking Arts damage part of this Talent improvement can vary with S3 use, as it does a variable number of hits depending on whether Yato2 can keep hitting enemies, however in general the damage improvement ends up being quite potent even when compared to her first Module. However, on S2, where Yato2 can max out this bonus (and S2 contains a multiplier to her Talent 1), the improvement is staggeringly large. As if this wasn’t enough, the buy one get one free deployment for the first time is another great bonus.

All these upgrades add up to make Yato2’s damage quite impressive despite her age as a unit. This second Module, Module Y, is almost always better than her first, Module X. Her first Module should only be considered where DP economy is tight, while this one should be the default. 


#### Narantuya

<ModuleRecommendation moduleType="Y" charId="char_4138_narant" recommended={"maybe"} recommendedLevel={3} client:load/>

Narantuya’s first Module leaned into the “attacking close enemies” gimmick of Loopshooter Snipers, and thus also substantially synergized with her S3. Meanwhile, this second Module, Module Y, is clearly intended for the opposite extreme. This Module leans into the long range of Loopshooters and rewards Narantuya for not getting hit, and doesn’t have conditions requiring her to hit nearby enemies.

The Trait upgrade of this Module grants Narantuya 1 SP for every 5 attacks she performs. This improvement doesn’t end up being particularly impactful most of the time. Narantuya’s S1 is ready almost immediately, while her S2, an Offensive Recovery skill, gets an effective SP reduction of 2, down to 13 attacks instead of 15 needed to fully charge it. Her S3 has the largest SP cost in her kit, of 30, and thus can benefit the most from this Trait, especially while enemies are nearby, but its cycle was already fairly fast and thus this Trait upgrade is generally not the star of the show for this Module. 

The Talent upgrade of this Module improves Narantuya’s Talent 2. The magnitude of the Dodge % remains the same, however the enemy accuracy debuff against nearby enemies is increased to 30% (up from 20%). This can not only make keeping Narantuya alive slightly easier, but it flows neatly into the brand new upgrade introduced by this Talent upgrade. Additionally, if Narantuya hasn’t been hit in the last 6 seconds, she gets +15% ATK. The extra dodge can sometimes be useful for helping Narantuya survive some extreme scenarios, but dedicated players are often willing to grind out the RNG anyway. Meanwhile, that extra +15% ATK is the real star of the show here. It’s not a massive buff, but none of her skills have +ATK% buffs therefore the difference is significant.

Both this Module (Module Y) and Narantuya’s first (Module X) have conditional damage. The first provides a higher ceiling of ATK and DEF steal, however Narantuya needs to hit enemies enough times to accomplish that ceiling. The first also provides an ATK% buff to Narantuya, but only when attacking nearby enemies. Meanwhile, this Module has nearly the opposite condition. Module Y wants Narantuya to avoid getting hit. Her Dodge and enemy accuracy debuff can help accomplish this even against nearby enemies, but the most reliable way to ensure the uptime of this condition is to attack enemies further away. As a result, her first Module leans much more into her S3 usage, which is most optimal in the same conditions (attacking nearby enemies). Meanwhile, this Module is much more synergistic when used for Narantuya’s S1 or S2, which both work much better at range than her S3. Ultimately there’s not a clean answer as to which Module is “better”, it largely depends on how players want to use Narantuya. Those who want to lean into the close range attacker nature of Loopshooters should probably stick to Module X. Players who want to take advantage of the large Loopshooter range or use Narantuya S2 should probably build this Module. While those who want to maximize their Narantuya usage can probably justify building both.


#### Vulpisfoglia

<ModuleRecommendation moduleType="Y" charId="char_4026_vulpis" recommended={false} client:load/>

Vulpisfoglia had the misfortune of releasing alongside a Module that is flat out the worse of the two Pioneer Vanguard Modules. This Module is the long-awaited “good” Pioneer Module that gives her the reduced DP cost and increases her global DP generation Talent.

The Trait upgrade of this Module reduces Vulpisfoglia’s DP cost by 4 for her first deployment. This upgrade is simple and great. Her first Module gives Vulpisfoglia some conditional stat buffs, but the conditions are not synergistic with her kit, and thus the theoretical damage buffs often don’t materialize. Meanwhile, getting to deploy a Vanguard earlier for the first time is simply nearly always a win.

The Talent upgrade of this Module improves her DP generation buff. Vulpisfoglia now buffs Natural DP generation by +15% (increased from 10%) while she is on the field. Furthermore, upon deployment (before her skills are activated) she gains Camouflage. The improvement to her DP generation is fairly modest, but isn’t reliant on her skill timings, unlike other Vanguards who often have to balance the combat utility of their skills with the desire to generate DP as early as possible. The extra Camouflage is generally not very impactful, only protecting Vulpisfoglia for a few seconds, until her S3 can be activated and grant her semi-permanent Camouflage.

This Module exchanges Vulipisfoglia’s combat capability for making her a far more competitive Vanguard. The difference between being able to deploy a Vanguard for 14 DP and 10 DP can often be staggering, while the combat performance of a Vanguard deployed that early is generally far less important. The extra combat capabilities Vulpisfoglia gets from her first Module may allow her to remain deployed and useful for longer, but for Vanguards, simply being deployed earlier and generated more DP makes this Module shine far more often. The biggest downside of this Module is simply that the Vanguard space has become quite crowded since Vulpisfoglia released, with multiple powerful feature-packed options competing for limited player resources and squad space. As a result, even though this Module is the better of the two for Vulpisfoglia, she may not be worth investing in regardless.
